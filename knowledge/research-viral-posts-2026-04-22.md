# VIRAL POST BREAKDOWNS WITH HOOKS

## How to Read These

For each post, note:
- **The Hook** (first 1-2 lines) — this is what made people stop scrolling
- **The Structure** — what came after the hook
- **Why It Worked** — the principle at play
- **Platform** — X (single), X (thread), LinkedIn
- **Engagement Level** — estimated from account size and typical reach

---

## HOOK TYPE 1: Counterintuitive / "Everyone Is Wrong"

### Example 1: The "X is Dead" Hook
> "Cold email is dead. Here's why you're still doing it wrong."

**Structure:**
1. Hook: Contrarian claim (bold, direct)
2. Body: 3 specific reasons why (numbered)
3. Evidence: Specific example or data point
4. Pivot: What to do instead

**Why it works:** Challenges reader's existing belief → creates tension → must read to resolve → shares because they want to prove the poster wrong OR agree and feel smarter

**Engagement pattern:** High reply volume, moderate likes, high share
**Platform:** X thread, LinkedIn post
**Best for:** People with strong opinions and evidence to back them up

---

4. Framework: Simple reframe

**Why it works on LinkedIn:** Client quote = social proof, first 3 lines create curiosity, professional context

## HOW TO USE THIS IN PRISM

Every time PRISM generates a hook, it should:

1. **Classify the hook type** (from the 20 formulas above)
2. **Check against what we know works** (from this research)
3. **Default to specificity** — specific numbers, specific outcomes, specific timeframes
4. **Include the "why it works"** in the draft notes so the user understands

**The strongest hooks stack 2 types:**
- Specific numbers + contrarian claim
- Vulnerability + actionable framework
- Authority credential + specific finding
